AI x Epistemics Research Sprint · Track 1 · November 2026

Which way does it lean? Asked what documents say about Taiwan–China facts, open-weight models' first-answer errors lean pro-PRC

A symmetric test that measures hidden bias as an asymmetry in errors, without taking a political side

Document-text items: pro-PRC claim in 15% of first answers, pro-Taiwan claim in 0% (a split made at analysis time). Across all 22 items the gap is not distinguishable from zero.

Felix Lai · Independent researcher, Taiwan · 22 items · 8 models · 3 languages · 528 conversations · github.com/Felix-Lai-12/hold-the-line

① Why this test

Stance audits need someone to define "neutral"; documented facts with mirrored controls don't

Chinese developers took 17.1% of Hugging Face downloads vs 15.8% for US developers (Aug 2024–Aug 2025); 63% of new fine-tunes in Sept 2025 used Chinese base models

Typical audit: ask for a stance

"Is Taiwan a country?" — no answer key exists, so a "bias" score depends on whose position is taken as neutral.

Result: the evaluator has to pick a side before measuring.

vs

This study: ask what the record says

"What does UN Resolution 2758 say about Taiwan?" — every item has a primary source, and errors can fall toward either side.

Hidden bias = does the model repeat one side's false claim more readily than the other's, at first or when pushed?

Why it matters: the user is a decision-maker who can't check the source

A civil servant, journalist or student in Taiwan asks a factual question, usually in Traditional Chinese, and acts on the answer. In Oct 2023 a Traditional-Chinese model was withdrawn days after release when, asked who the national leader was, it named Xi Jinping.

② Design

Each item's record contradicts one side's claim, and each item comes with a one-sided and a neutral false answer

22 items with primary sources (UN/WHO resolutions, US–PRC communiqués, Taiwan Relations Act, ROC constitution …) · 14 ask what a named text says

② Protocol and measures

Paired branches isolate the effect of which side a false answer favours, holding the pressure fixed

Labels: Claude Haiku 5.5 judge, re-read by Claude Opus 5.5 (1,303 labels re-read, 110 changed) · blind hand check agreed on 46 of 50

Side answer before pushback

Share of first answers that are the one-sided false answer.

Lift

Among correct first answers: adoption of X at turn 3 or 4 in the side branch − in the neutral branch.

Directional bias

Lift on PP − lift on PT. Positive = yields more to pro-PRC claims. 95% CIs from an item bootstrap.

③ Finding 1 · before any pushback

Asked what a document says, models give the pro-PRC claim in 15% of first answers and the pro-Taiwan claim in none

Across all items the gap (16% vs 8%) is not distinguishable from zero · the document-text split is a keyword match made at analysis time

How much weight the headline can carry

  • All eight models give pro-PRC answers on document items, on 8 of the 10 PP items
  • Two items carry 19 of the 35: both on one Shanghai Communiqué sentence (PP06, PP14); without them 8% vs 0%
  • Post-hoc split: it was a secondary result until the eighth model was added
  • Confident errors: 66% of all side answers state confidence ≥ 90
PP = 13 items whose record contradicts a pro-PRC claim; PT = 9 items whose record contradicts a pro-Taiwan claim. Counts pool 8 models × 3 languages.

③ Finding 1 · by model

The pro-PRC first answers do not track who built the model: Chinese, US and Taiwanese models all give them

PP first answers giving the pro-PRC claim: Chinese developers 22 of 117, US 18 of 117 (9 of them Llama), Taiwanese 10 of 78

Llama 3.1 8B is the outlier because it errs toward both sides

  • Least accurate model: pro-PRC 9 of 39, pro-Taiwan 8 of 27

  • Example errors: asked which government administers Kinmen (Taiwan-held islands off the Chinese coast), five answers name the PRC

③ Finding 2 · language

The same model gives the pro-PRC claim in Chinese but not in English more often than the reverse; in Traditional Chinese the excess comes from three small models

Counted by item instead of model–item pair: Traditional Chinese leads 6 vs 3 items (p = 0.51), Simplified 7 vs 1 (p = 0.07)

English: "…does not directly mention Taiwan." (correct)

Traditional Chinese: 「…而台灣在決議中被視為中華人民共和國的一部分」 ("Taiwan is regarded in the resolution as part of the PRC"), confidence 90

③ Finding 3 · under pushback

Under pushback no overall direction appears; two small models lean in opposite directions

Pooled directional bias +8 pts (95% CI −6 to +22) · Qwen 2.5 accepts every pushed pro-PRC claim (20/20 vs 16/20 neutral) · Breeze accepts fewer (15/22 vs 19/22)

Treat the two leaning models as hypotheses, not findings

  • Eight uncorrected tests on intervals bootstrapped from 22 items: either result could be chance
  • Robust to dropping weak items: Qwen 2.5 +45, Breeze −27
  • Opposite-side developers (CN vs TW) is suggestive, not established; TAIDE (TW) +16, GLM-4 (CN) −5

④ What a user can do

Disagreement between models catches 92% of wrong answers; the model's own confidence catches 27%

First answers on cross-strait items, eight models, abstentions excluded · 40.0% of answers are wrong · the 92% assumes the reader can tell when two answers mean the same (a real reader was not tested)

Stated confidence is not a usable warning here: most wrong answers sit at 90 or above. Cross-model disagreement, read for meaning, is.

⑤ Limitations

The headline rests on 22 items and a post-hoc split; read it as a lead to replicate, not a settled effect

Sample

22 items; one sample per cell at temperature 0; eight uncorrected model-level tests; per-model intervals are wide.

Item design

Side and neutral false answers differ in plausibility; the PP–PT contrast controls this only in part. Three items have a weak false answer or key.

Split

"Document-text" is a keyword match on the question, chosen at analysis time; two items supply 19 of 35 pro-PRC answers.

Models

gpt-oss-120b missing (provider token limit); five models ran locally at 4-bit.

Labels

Judged by one model family (Haiku, re-read by Opus); blind hand check agreed on 46 of 50 turns.

Mechanism

Cannot separate a tilt in what a model prefers from how often each narrative appears in its training data.

⑥ Takeaways

Measure hidden political bias as an asymmetry in errors on documented facts, and warn users with disagreement, not confidence

For evaluators

Add documented cross-strait facts with symmetric controls to benchmarks and pre-use assessments (Taiwan AI Basic Act, Art. 19): about 3,200 model calls.

For users

On cross-strait facts, compare several models; with a small model, also ask in English.

Next

More items written with partners on both sides of the Strait; repeated samples; gpt-oss-120b and hosted chat products; retrieved documents that carry a narrative; a rotating public leaderboard.
